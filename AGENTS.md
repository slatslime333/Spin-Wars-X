# Spin Wars X

Vanilla HTML/CSS/JS Beyblade X–style stadium game. There is no package manager or build step.

## Cursor Cloud specific instructions

- Serve the repo root over HTTP (`python3 -m http.server 8080`) and open `index.html`. Scripts are cache-busted (`style.css?v=8.37`, `xrail-engine.js?v=8.37`, `app.js?v=8.37`, `movement-engine.js?v=8.37`); hard-refresh after pulling.
- Blade art is optional. If `BLADE_ENGINE` has a `sprite` path, combo cards and the battle model use that PNG; otherwise the gold/silver circle stays. Phoenix Wing stays on `assets/blades/phienix_wing1.png`. Other blades map to the PNGs in `assets/blades/` (Leon Claw uses `Leonfang.png`). Shelter Drake and Dran Sword still have no art. Battle sprites are sized to the circle (`r=4.85`) and spin clockwise from live RPM until 0. Live battle Beys draw a faint, short (~220ms) gold/slate motion trail under the model so the path is readable on the light bowl; it is visual-only and does not change physics.
- Bit art lives in `assets/blades/png bit/`. Flat, Rush, Point, Hexa, Level, and Wedge show on bit pick cards and the VS combo plates. Other bits keep the PART IMAGE placeholder.
- Blade pick cards tint by `tier` (gold / silver / bronze). Home is a title screen: SPIN WARS with a green X badge, then a vertical league list (Bronze / Silver / Gold / Custom) with short pool copy. Combo check is the VS plates plus LET IT RIP; plates show boxed OVR and META plus boxed HIT/HOLD/MOVE stats. Battle chrome is commentary, then stadium, then names with ratchet · bit name (text only), RPM with a META box on the health bar row, then launch controls in `#launchDock`. The scoreboard is `0 VS 0` with `first to 7` underneath.
- If a requested feel tweak can be done by retuning an existing system, retune that system. Do not add another movement, knockback, or rail layer on top.
- Drop launches hang **under the top X-Rail, beside the X-Exit** (not on the rail lip and not inside the V), then shoot straight toward stadium middle. Launch quality is that shot's accuracy (Perfect is tight, Horrible is wide). Stadium side still picks left vs right. Rail contact is skipped only while the drop is stalling.
- The seven combo stats refine existing battle terms; OVR and META stay display-only. Battle uses each stat against the opponent's matching number, not against 99: 70 vs 70 plays at the same intensity as 94 vs 94, and 99 knock vs 88 defense should feel like 60 knock vs 50 defense. The ratio is soft-capped so a 99 vs 75 is favored, not a bye. Attack converts contact into RPM loss. Knockback converts contact into shove inside cap `0.086`. Defense absorbs RPM loss and slightly resists knock. Stamina slows idle spin drain. Balance holds stability after hits. Mobility slightly raises cruise/launch response, not orbit width. Burst is unused in battle. Bit, launch, and physics still decide the round.
- Knockback is the existing clash bounce: incoming momentum plus Knockback/Attack stats. Cancel the approaching contact, then bounce that component back so a head-on clash does not zero both Beys. Keep each Bey's own orbit tangent so they bounce and settle instead of swapping spin direction or following the same line. Always un-overlap even during the hit lock so they cannot phase through. Hits should shove with real weight (cap `0.086`, still below the `0.128` stadium launch) without skating the bowl like air hockey. Attack-type bits shove non-attack bits harder (`1.24`) and take less tank recoil (`0.82`); same-type clashes keep the shared bounce. A free X-Exit swing into a clash adds a light `1.11` shove. Live Defense fades with remaining RPM so dying Beys take a little more knock and RPM loss instead of chip-stalling. `impactMomentumState` follows the shove that landed on that Bey (not its own outgoing knock) but must not freeze orbit off — ease back into the ring (soft bowl) even while they are still sliding. Speed-target damping must not eat impact velocity while `impactMomentumState` is high. Separating overlaps still must not stack.
- Xtreme/Over need a real knock into the opening (`lastImpactForce` plus speed/alignment), not a graze or a missed X-Exit dump. A committed smash should usually stay scored. High-RPM X-Rail / X-Exit carries into the pocket should usually climb (~90%+ at 55% RPM) — that self-KO save does not apply to an opponent smash just because they touched the rail this lap. Low-RPM self-entries into Over/Xtreme are much easier to pocket. An X-Exit swing that then clashes gets a slight knock boost (`1.11`, still capped at `0.086`). An opponent hit after leaving the rail can still pocket. Show RECOVERED over IMPACT/launch callouts when it happens.
- Live orbit follows Bit type, then stats. Home radius is a soft radial tendency, not a locked path. Free movement is a cartesian blend toward launch+RPM speed (`physicalSpeedTarget`) with a soft bowl toward home radius. Attack is slightly inside the rail at full RPM. By 50% RPM it sits just under the X-Rail/X-Exit so laps miss the ring. By the last 20% RPM Attack pulls into a tight ring like Non-Attack, with a little leftover aggression. Tired Attack bits must actually walk in — leftover rail-width speed is bled when they are outside home. Orbit must yield to knockback the same way Defense/Stamina do. Non-Attack Center launches start a bit wide (under the X-Rail, never a rail sling) then walk in quickly; Ball/Orb stay shorter and tighter than Point/Level. After a smash they can be moved off that ring toward Over/Xtreme without skating like a hockey puck, then settle. Do not polar-rewrite heading into a perfect circle. X-Exit and free hits on the closer bounce toward center with most leftover speed and a slightly stronger shove to middle. While `railEngaged`, ride speed is owned by the X-Rail engine; do not cruise-damp it. Cruise accelerates up to `physicalSpeedTarget` and only bleeds excess; skip it while impact/exit is hot.
- X-Exit leaves the rail toward left-center, center, or right-center with a small heading change. Do not force every exit down the exact middle. A free Bey that hits the X-Exit bounces toward stadium center with most of its speed and a slightly stronger shove to middle; it rides only if the existing hook already has a real CCW bite and momentum into the rail.
- Only the true pocket mouths and Xtreme chute can be left by a real outward smash. The rest of the lower X-Rail and wall stay solid. Light taps still should not score. A finish must actually cross the zone, not just graze the lip.
- X-Rail capture uses remaining CCW + inward bite, not peak RPM. A real entry still has to hook; the bite/speed bar is a little easier so near-misses ride instead of bouncing. Failed hooks bounce off the rail softer and keep a little CCW so a high-RPM Attack can still wind on instead of skating straight off. Low remaining RPM raises that hook bar so tired wide laps bounce instead of riding out the match. X-Exit closer still sends them toward middle with some orbit tangent. Ride speed is the speed they arrived with plus a light RPM drive. Exit speed is that carried rail speed.
- Direct Clash aims at the opponent after both Beys spawn. Launch quality is aim accuracy (Perfect is tight, Horrible is wide). `impactMomentumState` stays high so they fly at each other instead of immediately orbiting.
- Center launches stay in the middle of the bowl but sit slightly toward that player's stadium side so two Center picks cannot spawn on top of each other. Attack-type bits still wind out toward their wide ring. Non-Attack bits wind into their own tighter orbit and must not sling out to the X-Rail like Attack. A small spin-correct tangent, not a random throw.
- X-Rail launches still start at the live lower corner via `SpinWarsXRailEngine.nearest`. Do not add a second invisible wall.
- CPU launch is locked at round start from the player's past techniques/tilts, never the live pick. Center-heavy habits get more X-Rail/Drop/Clash; X-Rail habits get rail contests and clashes, not Center; Clash habits get Center/rail/drop answers; Drop habits get Center and clash. CPU also avoids repeating itself.
- Lint/test/build: there is no project linter or test runner. Prove changes with a browser battle (Drop, Direct Clash, and X-Rail) plus any one-off `node` geometry asserts against `xrail-engine.js`.
